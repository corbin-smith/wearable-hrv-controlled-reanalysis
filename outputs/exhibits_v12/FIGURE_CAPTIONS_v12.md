# Figure captions, v12 (draft; numbers filled from the rerun files)

**Figure 1. Heart rate and the group differences.**
(A) RMSSD (logarithmic axis) and (B) sample entropy against mean heart rate for 60 HRV-ACC recordings. Both fall as
heart rate rises within each group (Spearman ρ with heart rate: RMSSD −0.654 in controls and −0.368 in patients; sample entropy −0.456 in controls and −0.443 in patients).
At a given heart rate patients' RMSSD is lower than controls', while their sample entropy is not.
(C) Each of 21 measures' Spearman correlation with group before (open grey circles) and after (dark circles) holding mean
heart rate constant; bars are 95% bootstrap intervals for the adjusted value. Filled dark circles remain significant after
Benjamini–Hochberg correction across the 21 adjusted tests (15 of 21).
RMSSD moves from −0.629 to −0.492.
The 6 measures that are not significant after adjustment (sample and approximate entropy and the four recurrence
measures) fall to between −0.152 and +0.023.

**Figure 3. Recording resolution.**
(A) RMSSD expressed in steps of a 7.8125 ms grid, the spacing of R-peak times in a 128 Hz recording (RMSSD at 1.000 ms
divided by 7.8125 ms). Median 1.22 steps in patients and 2.87 in controls. Horizontal bars are
medians; points are spread horizontally only to avoid overlap.
(B) Rank-biserial r for each measure at the recorded 1.000 ms resolution (open grey circles) and with the beat times of
the same recordings rounded to the 7.8125 ms grid (dark circles; each recording's value is its median over 10 offsets of
the sampling clock; bars are 95% bootstrap intervals). On that grid a median of 73% of patients' three-beat
patterns contain a tie (controls 39%). For the six amplitude measures, the two ratios and both slopes, r changes
by at most 0.078 (LF/HF ratio). Sample entropy moves from −0.340 to +0.136, approximate entropy from
−0.387 to +0.136, permutation entropy from +0.400 to −0.351 and DFA α₁ from −0.120 to −0.473. Grey diamonds show the simpler model in
which each interval, not each beat time, is rounded. Full-range DFA α was not recomputed. Values and intervals are in Table 4.

**Figure 2. Power spectra and the frequency range of the slope fit.**
(A) R-R interval power spectra for each recording (thin lines) and the group means (thick lines), in ms²/Hz on
logarithmic axes, shown to 0.5 Hz. Shading marks the Welch bins used for the slope fit (0.016–0.148 Hz; bins are
0.0078 Hz apart); hatching marks the 0.07–0.13 Hz band removed for the excised slope. Dashed lines have each group's
median uncorrected slope (controls 1.04, patients 1.81) and are a visual guide only.
(B) The uncorrected slope fitted over four ranges, mean ± SD, with the rank-biserial r for each (positive = steeper in
patients): 0.016–0.148 Hz +0.667; 0.008–0.148 Hz +0.651; 0.016–0.148 Hz with linear
interpolation +0.658; full spectrum 0.008–0.398 Hz −0.127. n = 30 controls and 30 patients.

**Figure 4. The MIT-BIH reference cohort.**
Each subject's spectral slope with 0.07–0.13 Hz excised (A) and RMSSD (B) in 18 MIT-BIH recordings, 30 HRV-ACC controls
and 30 HRV-ACC patients. Bars are medians (slope 1.21, 1.24 and
1.73; RMSSD 29.9, 22.4 and 9.5 ms); points are
spread horizontally only to avoid overlap. With age held constant, the slope differs between MIT-BIH controls and
patients (partial ρ = +0.557, p < 0.001) but not between the two
control cohorts (partial ρ = +0.039, p = 0.797);
positive r means higher in the HRV-ACC group.
MIT-BIH beat times are on a 7.8125 ms grid, and the recordings differ from HRV-ACC in device, setting and age.
